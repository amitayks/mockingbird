The Movement

Synthwave is a visual and musical genre born of nostalgia for the 1980s that never quite was — the neon-drenched sci-fi imagined by Blade Runner, Tron, and Miami Vice, rebuilt by digital artists a generation later. It runs on saturated sunsets, infinite wireframe horizons, and the hum of analog synthesizers.

Design Language

Six Principles

What separates authentic synthwave from a purple gradient. Every choice serves one goal: the memory of a future dreamed in 1984.

A

Sunset Over Everything

The palette is a sky at dusk — deep violet at the zenith, hot magenta at the band, molten orange at the horizon. Backgrounds are never flat; they glow from below.

B

Perspective Grid

Ground planes recede to a vanishing point with scanline horizons. Depth is drawn, not implied. Motion pushes the grid toward the viewer.

C

Glow Is Structure

Neon isn't decoration — it's how edges exist. Text, lines, and borders carry a colored bloom. Nothing is a flat 1px line; everything radiates.

D

Analog Artifacts

VHS scanlines, chromatic aberration, tape warble, grain. The imperfection of old media is the texture that sells the era.

E

Chrome & Extrusion

Display type is heavy and metallic — outlined, mirrored, beveled. Fonts like Monoton and geometric sans carry the retro-tech signal.

F

Iconic Silhouettes

Palms, sports cars, DeLoreans, distant cities, a single setting sun. Black cutouts against the blaze of the sky do the storytelling.

Origins

About the Style

Synthwave grew from the mid-2000s French electro scene into a full aesthetic movement, fusing electronic music with the visual vocabulary of 80s cinema and early computer graphics.

Timeline

  • 1982

    The Source Material

    Blade Runner and Tron define the neon-noir future the movement later mourns.

  • 2005

    French Roots

    Producers like Kavinsky and the Valerie collective revive analog synths and VHS visuals.

  • 2011

    Drive

    The film's soundtrack detonates the aesthetic into the mainstream — hot pink script, chrome, and all.

  • 2016

    Retro Everywhere

    Stranger Things and Hotline Miami cement synthwave as the default look of 80s nostalgia.

  • Now

    A Living Language

    Grids, sunsets and glow are a permanent design dialect — reused, remixed, and shown here.
